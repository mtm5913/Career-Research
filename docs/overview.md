The purpose is to compare how UX research, forward deployed engineering, and AI product engineering use AI, then identify a provisional career direction and practical development priorities.

## 3. What the comparison shows

### UX research using AI

The center of the work is planning studies, interpreting evidence, and communicating findings. AI supports the research process. This direction aligns most closely with my interest in understanding people's behavior.

[Explore UX research and the Deltek reference →](./roles/ux-research)

### Forward deployed engineering

The center of the work is understanding a customer's workflow and delivering a working technical solution. The Provectus reference adds programming, integration, evaluation, and deployment expectations.

[Explore FDE and the Provectus reference →](./roles/fde)

### AI product engineering

The center of the work is turning user needs into working products across interfaces and services. The Anthropic reference connects design judgment to implementation, agent reliability, and iteration with external users.

[Explore AI product engineering and the Anthropic reference →](./roles/product-engineering)

**Provisional choice:** UX research involving AI-enabled products or AI-assisted research workflows. Technical learning can help me understand system limits and collaborate with engineers. Deltek is a UX research alternative; using AI in research does not make it retrieval engineering or model evaluation.

## Peer feedback: homepage readability — November 16, 2026

[GitHub issue #1](https://github.com/mtm5913/Career-Research/issues/1) records feedback received from a peer and posted by the project owner because the peer could not post directly. The peer reported crowded homepage headline letters/words and process text too small to read: “read → compare → question → choose a next step”. The peer was not identified by name in the supplied feedback.

**Decision and changed documentation:** Accept the feedback. Remove negative tracking from the homepage headline and its italic text, add a little word spacing, and use 1.25 line spacing. Increase the process text from 8px desktop / 6px mobile to 14px at both sizes with 1.7 line spacing. Let the card grow and its content flow vertically so wrapped text does not overlap the note. Preserve the wording and site colors. This section records the feedback, decision, change, checks and next step in the existing research record.

**Actual checks:** AI-assisted review of local production builds at 1440 × 1000 and 390 × 844. Both headlines use normal letter spacing; process text computes to 14px. No document-level horizontal overflow was observed (1425px content at a 1440px viewport; 375px at a 390px viewport). The process text wraps on mobile and stays inside its card, below the note. Both headlines and process cards were also visually inspected in Chrome at actual 200% page zoom; content reflowed and the reviewed text remained visible without overlap. Chrome zoom was reset afterward. Production build and internal-link checks passed before adding this review section (353 internal references); final release checks are attached to the issue.

[View the updated desktop homepage screenshot](/peer-review/homepage-desktop.png).

**Help and limits:** AI made the CSS changes, drafted this record and performed the browser/build checks at Megan’s request. The peer supplied the original feedback; these follow-up checks are not a peer retest or a comprehensive accessibility audit. No new employer-source review or RAG answer-quality test is claimed by this visual change.

**Peer follow-up — November 16, 2026:** Megan relayed the peer’s response to the updated homepages: “Yeah that looks much better”. This confirms the peer perceived an improvement after the typography changes. The response did not specify a device, browser, zoom level, or separate test of each text element, so those details are not claimed.

**Decision / next:** Keep the revised typography. Peer confirmation is now recorded; no additional change was requested in this response. Deployment and issue-resolution evidence remains in the linked GitHub issue. Recheck readability if the homepage layout or wording changes.
