# Making room for intentional use

How can a social app help people act on their intentions rather than slip into automatic use?

## Context

Our group studied social media usage and intent through diary-study analysis. The shared report combines qualitative findings, descriptive usage data, integrated insights, and design opportunities. It discusses four participants; the findings are exploratory and do not represent all social media users.

## My contribution

**I created all eight design opportunities based on the group's findings.** The underlying study and findings were group work. I do not claim sole ownership of data collection, charts, or the full analysis.

## From observation to opportunity

| Pattern in the group analysis | My design opportunity | What a future test could ask |
|---|---|---|
| Opening an app automatically | A launch prompt asking what the person intends to do | Does the prompt help without adding unwanted friction? |
| Losing track of time | A visible session timer or reminder | Can people notice and understand elapsed time? |
| Rechecking after notifications | Controls for timing, frequency, and importance | Can people configure interruptions to match their needs? |
| Difficulty stopping | Reduce additional recommendations after a task | Does a stopping point support the user's own goal? |

## The eight opportunities

1. Encourage intentional app use with a launch-time purpose prompt.
2. Increase screen-time awareness through timers or reminders.
3. Reduce endless scrolling after the original task is complete.
4. Allow more notification customization.
5. Offer post-session reflection or mood check-ins.
6. Explore less disruptive advertisement placements.
7. Improve content transparency, including AI-content labeling or screening.
8. Include more informational content to support purposeful use.

## Decision and limits

These ideas translate observations into possible features. They were **not implemented or validated**. In particular, AI-content labeling is a proposal, not an AI system I built. The report's explanations are hypotheses about behavior, not causal proof.

## What I would test next

Prototype one intention prompt, compare it with a version without the prompt, and observe whether people can complete their intended task. I would record confusion and unwanted interruptions, not assume that a reminder is helpful.

*Evidence basis: Group 6 Diary Study Analysis, supplied for portfolio preparation. Raw participant data and the shared class document are not published here.*
