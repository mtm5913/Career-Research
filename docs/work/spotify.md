# What makes Premium worth it?

Two listeners, different tolerances: exploring the tradeoffs behind Spotify Free and Premium.

## Research question

What influences whether a listener considers Premium worth paying for? Our group considered listening habits, frustrations, feature value, and subscription decisions.

## My contribution and method

I contributed a structured question guide, conducted **two semi-structured interviews**—one Free listener and one Premium listener—and annotated the responses to identify themes. I asked about routines, music discovery, subscription choices, ads, and skip limitations, with follow-up questions about their reasoning.

The group combined findings and proposed opportunities. My interview and annotation work is distinct from ownership of the complete group's conclusions.

## What these interviews revealed

| Topic | Free listener | Premium listener |
|---|---|---|
| Price | A barrier to upgrading | Willing to keep paying for valued convenience |
| Ads | Tolerable | A major reason to prefer Premium |
| Listening control | Limited skips caused frustration | Valued choosing music without interruption |
| Familiarity | Familiarity and social influence mattered | A trial and accumulated playlists supported continued use |

The contrast matters: one interruption or pricing message may not fit both listeners. These are observations from two people, not a claim about the whole Spotify audience.

## Analysis and opportunities

My transcript includes inline annotations and a theme summary. Our group discussed alternative freemium choices, referral trials, and feedback on generated playlists. Those were proposals; no resulting design was implemented or retested in these records.

## What I would improve

Use more neutral follow-ups, document recruitment and consent, and recruit a broader sample before making product recommendations. A future usability study would add evidence about observed behavior, rather than treating interview responses as proof of feature effectiveness.

## Evidence boundaries

This Spotify study is separate from Olive. Its findings were not used to build or revise that application.

The original coursework guide uses the word “surveys,” but the supplied transcripts establish interviews. I do not count them as two separate collection methods. Participant names and raw transcripts are omitted from this public case study.
